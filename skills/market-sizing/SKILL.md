# Market Sizing

## Mission

Produce a decision-useful range of opportunity size with assumptions exposed, not a large unsupported headline number.

## Prerequisites

- the product or service definition
- target geography, customer segment, time horizon, and available market evidence

## Procedure

1. Define the offer, paying customer, geography, use case, pricing unit, and time period. Clarify whether the estimate is annual revenue, users, units, or spend.
2. Define TAM, SAM, and SOM operationally for this specific market. Explain what is included and excluded in each boundary.
3. Build a bottom-up estimate from countable customers or units, realistic adoption, frequency, capacity, and price. Show each multiplication and source.
4. Build a top-down cross-check from authoritative market or industry statistics, adjusting for relevance, overlap, geography, and date.
5. Check for double counting across segments, channel partners, multi-product spend, and adjacent markets.
6. Separate market demand from the obtainable share. Model access to distribution, competitive response, sales capacity, adoption friction, and regulatory constraints.
7. Create low, base, and high cases with sensitivity to the drivers that dominate the result. Keep inputs ranges where evidence is weak.
8. Compare estimates with observed benchmarks and explain discrepancies. Use a reasonableness check against customer counts, existing spend, and capacity.
9. Present results with source dates, calculation steps, uncertainty, and the next primary research needed to validate the estimate.

## Output contract

- Market definition and TAM/SAM/SOM boundaries.
- Bottom-up calculation and independent top-down check.
- Source and assumption ledger.
- Low/base/high range and sensitivity drivers.
- Caveats and validation plan.

## Boundaries and stop conditions

- Never repeat a market report headline without checking its definition and scope.
- Do not present an obtainable share as guaranteed revenue.
- Do not fabricate market counts, conversion rates, prices, or citations.
- State when an estimate is too uncertain for a precise point value.

## Completion rule

Finish only when the requested output is delivered, important assumptions and limitations are visible, and the result has been checked against the output contract. Report what was and was not verified.
