# Knowledge Management

## Mission
Make organizational knowledge findable, trustworthy, maintainable, and appropriately accessible.

## Procedure
1. Identify decisions and recurring tasks the knowledge system should support. Use representative questions and observed search/support gaps.
2. Inventory collections, formats, owners, update frequency, access restrictions, duplication, and gaps. Distinguish authoritative records from informal notes.
3. Define a taxonomy reflecting user questions and organizational concepts. Prefer a small stable hierarchy with links and tags over deep folder trees.
4. Specify metadata: title, summary, owner, source, created/updated dates, scope, version, sensitivity, review date, and lifecycle status.
5. Create templates for procedures, decisions, policies, references, and troubleshooting. Keep landing pages concise and link to details.
6. Establish source-of-truth rules, content ownership, review cadence, stale-content signals, archival policy, and correction processes.
7. Design retrieval around exact terms, synonyms, metadata filters, and user intent. Require citations to source records for consequential answers.
8. Map access by role and sensitivity. Ensure indexing and summaries never expose material beyond source permissions.
9. Pilot with representative questions. Measure findability, correctness, stale-result rate, duplication, and time to locate authoritative sources.
10. Assign governance owners and feedback routes for missing, contradictory, or outdated information.

## Output contract
- Audience and knowledge-use cases.
- Source inventory and authority map.
- Taxonomy, metadata, and content templates.
- Ownership, review, archival, and access model.
- Pilot questions, success measures, and governance plan.

## Boundaries
- Do not ingest restricted or personal documents without authorization.
- Do not treat generated summaries as authoritative replacements for source records.
- Do not expose snippets, embeddings, or metadata beyond source permissions.
- Escalate conflicting policies or records to designated owners.

## Completion
Finish when users have a clear retrieval path, important sources have governance, and access/freshness risks are addressed.
