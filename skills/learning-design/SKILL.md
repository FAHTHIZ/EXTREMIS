# Learning Experience Design

## Mission

Create a coherent learning experience where activities and assessments directly support clearly stated learning outcomes.

## Prerequisites

- learner audience and context
- topic, learning time, delivery format, and any curriculum constraints

## Procedure

1. Describe the learners, prerequisite knowledge, context, constraints, access needs, and intended change in knowledge or performance.
2. Write observable learning outcomes using measurable verbs. Avoid vague outcomes such as 'understand' unless paired with evidence of understanding.
3. Choose the minimum content needed for those outcomes and sequence it from prerequisite concepts to more demanding applications.
4. Select learning activities that require learners to retrieve, explain, discuss, practise, create, or apply—not only consume information.
5. Design formative checks during the lesson and an assessment that directly measures each outcome. State what acceptable performance looks like.
6. Provide varied examples, accessible formats, clear language, and reasonable options for participation without lowering the learning objective.
7. Estimate timing realistically, including transitions, questions, practice, feedback, and breaks. Identify materials and facilitator preparation.
8. Pilot the experience or review it with learners and educators. Use observed learning evidence to revise confusing content or weak assessment alignment.

## Output contract

- Audience and prerequisite profile.
- Measurable learning outcomes.
- Lesson sequence with timing and activities.
- Aligned assessment and success criteria.
- Accessibility, materials, and revision notes.

## Boundaries and stop conditions

- Do not claim a lesson is effective without learner evidence.
- Do not design assessments that depend on irrelevant personal disclosures or inaccessible formats.
- Respect age, context, language, disability, and safeguarding requirements.
- Do not use AI-generated assessment scores as the sole high-stakes decision.

## Completion rule

Finish only when the requested output is delivered, important assumptions and limitations are visible, and the result has been checked against the output contract. Report what was and was not verified.
