# Literature Review

## Mission

Summarize what a body of literature supports, how strong the evidence is, and where uncertainty or disagreement remains.

## Prerequisites

- a research question or topic
- access to bibliographic records and full text where available

## Procedure

1. Translate the topic into a focused question with population or system, intervention or concept, comparison, outcome, and time scope where useful.
2. Write a search protocol before screening: databases or repositories, search terms, date range, language limits, inclusion and exclusion criteria.
3. Search primary literature and relevant reviews. Record query, source, search date, and result counts; do not imply exhaustive coverage if access is limited.
4. Screen titles, abstracts, and full texts against the stated criteria. Keep exclusion reasons and avoid silently changing criteria to fit a preferred conclusion.
5. Extract study design, sample or dataset, methods, outcomes, limitations, funding or conflicts where reported, and the exact claims supported.
6. Assess evidence quality and applicability. Distinguish randomized studies, observational work, simulations, qualitative research, preprints, reviews, and opinion.
7. Synthesize by themes, methods, and points of agreement or conflict. Do not combine incomparable results or perform a meta-analysis without appropriate data and expertise.
8. Identify evidence gaps, publication and selection bias, external-validity limits, and unanswered questions. Cite each substantive claim to its source.
9. Write a reproducible methods note and a balanced conclusion with confidence calibrated to the evidence.

## Output contract

- Review question and scope.
- Search strategy and inclusion/exclusion criteria.
- Evidence table with citations, methods, and limitations.
- Thematic synthesis, gaps, and calibrated conclusion.

## Boundaries and stop conditions

- Never fabricate papers, authors, DOI values, sample sizes, or findings.
- Clearly label preprints and non-peer-reviewed work.
- Do not call a review systematic unless the search and screening process actually meets that standard.
- Respect copyright; summarize rather than reproduce protected full text.

## Completion rule

Finish only when the requested output is delivered, important assumptions and limitations are visible, and the work has been checked against the output contract. Report what was and was not verified.
