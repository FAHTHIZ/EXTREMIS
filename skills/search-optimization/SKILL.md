# Search Optimization

## Mission
Improve useful, accessible content discovery by fixing technical barriers and matching pages to genuine user intent.

## Procedure
1. Clarify site purpose, audience, geography/language, goals, and important pages. Do not treat ranking as the only success measure.
2. Inspect representative URLs using authorized tools. Record status codes, redirects, canonicalization, robots directives, sitemap coverage, internal links, and indexability.
3. Review information architecture and internal linking. Ensure important pages are reachable and duplicates are handled deliberately.
4. Map search intent to pages. Check whether each page satisfies a real user task with original, accurate information.
5. Review titles, descriptions, headings, image alternatives, structured data, and language annotations. Keep markup consistent with visible content.
6. Assess mobile usability, performance, accessibility, rendering, and content stability. Distinguish field metrics from local tests.
7. Review backlinks and referrals for relevance and authenticity; do not equate link volume with quality.
8. Establish baselines for impressions, qualified visits, and conversions. Account for seasonality, tracking changes, and search volatility.
9. Prioritize improvements by user benefit, evidence, cost, and risk. Keep a change log.
10. Recheck representative pages and metrics after changes; state when observation time is too short for conclusions.

## Output contract
- Technical audit with affected URLs and evidence.
- Intent-to-page map and content-quality findings.
- Prioritized remediation backlog.
- Baseline metrics, monitoring plan, and verification notes.

## Boundaries
- Do not promise ranking positions or guaranteed traffic increases.
- Do not recommend cloaking, doorway pages, hidden text, fabricated reviews, or manipulative link schemes.
- Do not bypass access controls to inspect restricted content.
- Verify changing requirements against current authoritative documentation.

## Completion
Finish when technical issues and content gaps are prioritized, changes are tied to user value, and measurement limits are explicit.
