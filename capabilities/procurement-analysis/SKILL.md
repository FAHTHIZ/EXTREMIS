# Procurement Analysis

## Mission
Support transparent purchasing decisions by comparing supplier evidence, lifecycle costs, delivery constraints, and contract risks.

## Procedure
1. Define the need, required outcomes, scope, expected volumes, service levels, delivery timeline, and constraints. Separate mandatory requirements from preferences.
2. Establish evaluation criteria before scoring proposals. Consider capability fit, quality, total cost, implementation, support, security/privacy, resilience, accessibility, sustainability, and exit options.
3. Normalize supplier responses so features, quantities, terms, currencies, and time periods are comparable.
4. Build a weighted scorecard with rationale for each weight. Keep pass/fail requirements separate from preference scoring.
5. Estimate total cost of ownership across acquisition, integration, training, operations, support, upgrades, usage growth, switching, and exit. Show uncertain inputs as ranges.
6. Assess delivery and dependency risks: supplier capacity, subcontractors, geographic constraints, continuity, data portability, lock-in, and single-supplier exposure.
7. Review contract scope, acceptance, change control, service levels, data handling, intellectual property, liability, renewal, termination, and dispute processes. Flag terms for qualified review.
8. Perform due diligence using authorized public and supplier-provided evidence. Distinguish verified facts, supplier claims, and unanswered questions.
9. Run sensitivities on price, volume, implementation delay, and service quality. Identify what would change the preferred option.
10. Present the shortlist, trade-offs, negotiation questions, approval gates, and implementation/exit plan.

## Output contract
- Requirements and evaluation criteria.
- Normalized supplier comparison and weighted scorecard.
- Total-cost model with assumptions and sensitivities.
- Risk register and due-diligence gaps.
- Recommendation, approval gates, and negotiation questions.

## Boundaries
- A scorecard does not replace procurement policy, legal review, or conflict-of-interest checks.
- Do not fabricate supplier capabilities, references, pricing, or certifications.
- Disclose uncertainty and conflicts in the evidence.
- Do not place orders, sign agreements, or disclose confidential bids without explicit authorization.

## Completion
Finish when comparisons are traceable to requirements and evidence, lifecycle costs are visible, and approval dependencies are clear.
