# Experimental Design

## Mission

Make the proposed method capable of answering the question while minimizing bias, risk, and unnecessary complexity.

## Prerequisites

- the research question and field context
- available equipment/data, relevant standards, and appropriate supervision for regulated work

## Procedure

1. Clarify the question, existing evidence, study type, target system or population, and what decision the result is meant to inform.
2. Write a falsifiable hypothesis and identify the primary outcome before selecting methods. Distinguish exploratory questions from confirmatory tests.
3. Define independent, dependent, control, and potential confounding variables. Specify operational measurements and units.
4. Choose an appropriate design: controlled experiment, observational study, repeated measures, simulation, field study, or qualitative approach. Explain why it fits.
5. Plan sampling, sample-size rationale, controls, randomization, blinding where feasible, replication, and exclusion rules. Do not invent statistical power.
6. Specify data collection, calibration, instrument uncertainty, missing-data handling, and a reproducible record of protocol deviations.
7. Assess ethical, environmental, and safety requirements before any activity. Obtain necessary approvals and qualified supervision before work involving people, animals, hazardous materials, pathogens, high energy, or other regulated risks.
8. Define the analysis plan, primary and secondary outcomes, stopping rules, and how negative or inconclusive findings will be reported.
9. Pilot only within approved safe conditions. Revise the protocol based on feasibility while documenting any changes from the original plan.

## Output contract

- Research question and falsifiable hypothesis.
- Study design, variables, controls, and sampling rationale.
- Measurement and data-management plan.
- Analysis and preregistration outline.
- Ethics, safety, approval, and limitation checklist.

## Boundaries and stop conditions

- Do not provide operational instructions for dangerous experiments or handling hazardous biological, chemical, radiological, or high-energy materials.
- Do not involve human or animal participants without required ethical review, consent, and qualified supervision.
- Do not manipulate methods or outcomes to obtain a preferred result.
- Do not imply that a proposed protocol has been approved or is safe merely because it is documented.

## Completion rule

Finish only when the requested output is delivered, important assumptions and limitations are visible, and the result has been checked against the output contract. Report what was and was not verified.
