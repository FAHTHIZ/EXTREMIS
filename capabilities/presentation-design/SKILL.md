# Presentation Design

## Mission

Help an audience understand and remember a key message through a coherent, honest, accessible visual story.

## Prerequisites

- audience, purpose, and time available
- source facts, data, or approved claims

## Procedure

1. Clarify audience knowledge, desired decision or learning outcome, presentation duration, delivery context, and constraints.
2. Write the central message in one sentence. Identify the few supporting points the audience must understand to act.
3. Build a narrative sequence: context, problem or opportunity, evidence, implications, recommendation or lesson, and next step.
4. Give each slide one main job and a message-led title. Remove slides that do not support the audience's decision or understanding.
5. Choose visuals that fit the evidence: diagrams for structure, charts for quantitative comparisons, tables for exact lookup, and images only when they add meaning.
6. Preserve chart integrity: units, denominators, time window, source, uncertainty, and honest scales. Avoid decorative data visuals.
7. Use readable typography, consistent spacing, accessible contrast, restrained motion, and visual hierarchy appropriate to the viewing distance.
8. Add speaker notes or references for claims that need context. Distinguish sourced facts from forecasts, assumptions, and recommendations.
9. Check the deck in presentation mode, including text overflow, image quality, reading order, links, and consistency. Test the final export when possible.

## Output contract

- Audience and core-message brief.
- Slide-by-slide narrative and content.
- Evidence-matched charts/diagrams with sources.
- Speaker notes or presenter guidance where needed.
- Final quality checklist and unresolved facts.

## Boundaries and stop conditions

- Do not invent metrics, customer logos, endorsements, or market claims.
- Do not distort charts to make a recommendation appear stronger.
- Do not overcrowd slides with tiny text to fit every detail.
- Use licensed or original visual assets and preserve attribution where required.

## Completion rule

Finish only when the requested output is delivered, important assumptions and limitations are visible, and the result has been checked against the output contract. Report what was and was not verified.
